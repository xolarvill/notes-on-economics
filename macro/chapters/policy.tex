\part{Money, Finance, and Macroeconomic Policy}\label{part:policy}

The earlier parts establish the real and nominal benchmarks. We now add
financial constraints and differences between households, then compare the
transmission and welfare effects of monetary and fiscal policy. Open economy
mechanisms remain part of that comparison.

\clearpage
\plannedsection{Money, Banking, and Central Bank Operations}\label{sec:money-banking}

\subsection{Money, payments, and financial claims}
Distinguish money from other assets and identify the services that give it a
role in household and firm decisions.

\subsection{Banks and balance sheets}
Set out the balance sheets of households, commercial banks, and the central
bank. Explain lending, deposits, reserves, and the limits to credit creation.

\subsection{Implementing monetary policy}
Connect the model's policy rate to the central bank's operating framework.
Distinguish reserve provision, asset purchases, and changes in borrowing costs.

\plannedsection{Financial Frictions and Aggregate Adjustment}\label{sec:financial-frictions}

\subsection{Borrowing limits and collateral}
Derive a simple borrowing constraint and show how it changes optimal choices.
Connect collateral values to spending and investment.

\subsection{Intermediation and financial amplification}
Introduce a constraint on intermediary balance sheets. Explain how losses,
leverage, and credit spreads can amplify a real or financial disturbance.

\subsection{Financial distress and policy intervention}
Distinguish liquidity problems from insolvency. State the mechanisms and
trade-offs behind liquidity support, restructuring, and macroprudential policy.

\clearpage
\plannedsection{Household Heterogeneity and Policy Transmission}\label{sec:heterogeneity}

\subsection{Income risk, insurance, and precautionary saving}
Introduce uninsurable income risk and borrowing limits. Explain how the
distribution of liquid wealth affects saving and consumption.

\subsection{Marginal propensities to consume and redistribution}
Compare households' spending responses to income and wealth changes.
Trace how taxes, transfers, and interest payments redistribute resources.

\subsection{From representative to heterogeneous households}
Use a two-agent benchmark before motivating a richer distribution of households.
Identify which policy questions require the additional heterogeneity.

retained below; the preceding sections are outlines.

\plannedsection{Fiscal Policy}\label{sec:fiscal-policy}

\subsection{Government budgets, debt, and Ricardian equivalence}
Derive the government flow and intertemporal budget constraints. Explain the
conditions for Ricardian equivalence and why they can fail.

\subsection{Taxes, purchases, transfers, and public investment}
Separate income, substitution, and resource effects. Compare the objectives
and transmission mechanisms of different fiscal instruments.

\subsection{Fiscal multipliers and financing}
Define the multiplier and its horizon. Compare spending responses across
financing arrangements, monetary policy rules, household structures, and
open economy environments.

\plannedsection{Policy Interaction and Constraints}\label{sec:policy-interaction}

\subsection{Fiscal and monetary regimes}
Study how the fiscal backing of public liabilities interacts with interest-rate
policy, inflation, and equilibrium determination.

\subsection{Debt sustainability and policy space}
Derive debt-to-output dynamics and distinguish arithmetic from an assessment of
sustainability. Include growth, interest rates, primary balances, and risk.

\subsection{Policy combinations in an open economy}
Bring together exchange-rate regimes, capital flows, domestic balance sheets,
and international spillovers. Compare policy combinations under stated
institutional and financial constraints.
