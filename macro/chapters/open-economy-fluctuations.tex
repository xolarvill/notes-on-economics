\plannedsection{Open Economy Fluctuations and Exchange Rates}\label{sec:open-fluctuations}

\subsection{The small open economy RBC model}
Extend the real benchmark to international borrowing and investment.
Compare productivity, world-interest-rate, and terms-of-trade shocks.

\subsection{Capital flows, interest parity, and risk}
Derive the relevant asset-pricing conditions and distinguish covered parity
from an uncovered expected-return condition. Introduce risk premia and
foreign-currency balance-sheet exposure.

\subsection{Nominal rigidities and exchange-rate regimes}
Introduce an open economy New Keynesian benchmark. Compare the adjustment
mechanisms under fixed and flexible exchange rates, and state the constraints
created by capital mobility.

\subsection{From open economy equilibrium to IS--LM--BP}
\label{sec:open-islmbp}

\textit{Planned bridge.} After the dynamic benchmarks, derive conditional goods,
money, and external-balance diagrams. Separate their fixed expectations from
the full equilibrium that determines those expectations.\footnote{A
reference for the classical framework and its extensions is Frenkel and Razin
(1987), \href{https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/024/1987/003/article-A001-en.xml}{\textit{The Mundell--Fleming Model: A Quarter Century Later}},
IMF Staff Papers 34(3), 567--620.}

The derivation will define the nominal exchange rate as domestic currency per
unit of foreign currency, and the real rate as $q=eP^*/P$. Trace how consumption,
investment, and net exports enter IS, how money demand enters LM, and which
trade elasticities are needed for depreciation to raise net exports. State the
short-run price assumptions and the treatment of initial external wealth.

For BP, first use the accounting sign convention in
Chapter~\ref{sec:international-accounts}. With no capital-account transfers or
measurement discrepancy, split the financial account into private transactions
and reserve acquisition. Net private financial inflows are the negative of the
private financial-account balance. A zero-reserve-change locus therefore sets
the current account plus net private inflows to zero. The balance of payments
already balances as an accounting system; BP is an additional external-market
closure, not an identity that determines a capital-flow response.

Compare three explicit cases: restricted capital flows, a finite interest-sensitive
flow schedule, and perfect asset substitutability. A finite capital-flow schedule
requires its own behavioral assumption or a model of portfolio costs, risk, or
constraints; it does not follow automatically from the household Euler equation.
Derive covered parity separately from uncovered expected-return conditions.
Only with the stated risk and exchange-rate expectations does the latter reduce
to equality of domestic and foreign interest rates and the familiar horizontal BP.

Compare monetary and fiscal experiments under an unsterilized peg and a float
with fixed reserves and given money. Paired $(Y,i)$ diagrams identify what
adjusts in each regime and the assumptions behind the policy rankings. Map
these conditional relations to Euler spending, interest parity, nominal
adjustment, and the monetary rule of an open economy NK model.
